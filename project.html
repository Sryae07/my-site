Things I might build

Five ideas, written down quickly before judging any of them. For each one: what it is, who it is for, and the four pages it would need. Then the one that survived the questions.

The five

  1. 1 · A desk for my AI tools

    Notes on the local AI stack I actually run: how the agent framework is installed, what broke, and the checks that prove it works.

    Who for
    Classmates who ask me how the setup works.
    Four pages
    Home · Setups · Troubleshooting · About
    Status
    Built as my personal project

  2. 2 · A portfolio that answers questions

    Four case studies instead of a list of skills: what the problem was, what I built, what I would change.

    Who for
    Anyone reading a CV and wondering whether the claims are true.
    Four pages
    Home · Work · Notes · Contact
    Status
    Merged into idea 1

  3. 3 · A survival guide for new students

    Where to eat, how the campus wifi works, which office answers which question — the page I wish I had in my first week.

    Who for
    Students arriving next semester.
    Four pages
    Home · Food · Campus · Contacts
    Status
    Later — needs photos I do not have yet

  4. 4 · A study timer that counts honestly

    Pomodoro-style sessions with a running total, so the number at the end of the week is real instead of remembered.

    Who for
    Me, during exam weeks.
    Four pages
    Home · Timer · History · Settings
    Status
    Later — needs storage, which the brief rules out

  5. 5 · A page that explains one bug

    One long-form page about a single hard bug: the wrong diagnosis, the evidence that settled it, and the fix.

    Who for
    Anyone who hits the same error message.
    Four pages
    Home · The bug · The fix · Notes
    Status
    Later — one page is not four

Which one, and why

The first two were really the same site seen from two angles, so I merged them: a portfolio whose case studies are the notes. That satisfies the four pages, gives me material I can defend without rehearsing, and keeps the smallest version worth showing to four pages.

The others were dropped for honest reasons rather than taste: idea 3 needs photographs I do not have, idea 4 needs to store data between visits and the brief says nothing may depend on that, and idea 5 does not survive the four-page test.
